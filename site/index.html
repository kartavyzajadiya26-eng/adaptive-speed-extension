What it does on a real web page

From the video's sound to its speed, 30 times a second

You don't press anything. Open a video on YouTube, a course site or a news page and the extension goes through these four steps on its own.

01 · Find

Spots every video

It watches the page for <video> players, including ones that appear later, like YouTube's next video or an embedded player.

02 · Listen

Hears speech on your device

A small speech-detection model (Silero VAD, about 1 MB) checks every 32 ms of sound: is someone talking? It runs in the browser's audio thread, so the page itself stays smooth.

03 · Decide

Waits for a real pause

A breath between words isn't a pause. Only after 0.6 s of silence does it speed up, and it learns each speaker's rhythm so it stops reacting to their usual short gaps.

04 · Play

Changes the speed

It eases up to 2.5× over a quarter of a second. The moment someone speaks it drops back to 1× and replays the first fraction of a second, so no word starts fast.

0.6 s

Silence needed before speeding up. Pause handling can be set to relaxed, balanced or aggressive.

0.2 s

How far it jumps back when speech starts during a fast stretch, so you hear the first syllable at 1×.

1× / 2.5×

Default speeds for talking and silence. Set both in the popup; you can also speed up talking itself.

Motion mode

Optional: when the picture is moving (a demo, a diagram being drawn) pauses only go to 1.6×, so you don't miss it.

Compatibility

Where it works

It works wherever the browser lets an extension hear a video's sound. When it can't hear, it leaves the speed alone rather than guessing.

Install in about a minute

Add it to Chrome

Adaptive Speed isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you add it in developer mode. It's the same extension, loaded from a folder.

  1. Download and unzip adaptive-speed-extension.zip. You get a folder with a manifest.json inside.
  2. Open chrome://extensions in a new tab
  3. Turn on Developer mode with the switch in the top-right corner.
  4. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped folder: the one that contains manifest.json, not a folder above it.
  5. Play any video. A small badge on the video shows the current speed. Click the extension's icon to change the speeds.

If Chrome says "Could not load manifest"

You picked the folder one level too high. Open it and select the folder that has manifest.json directly inside.

See what it's deciding

On a video page, open the console (Cmd + Option + J on Mac, Ctrl + Shift + J on Windows), paste this, then reload:

localStorage.adaptiveSpeedDebug = "1"

Once a second it prints whether it hears speech, how sure it is, and the current speed.

Privacy

The sound never leaves your computer

Everything happens inside your browser. There's no account, no server and no tracking.

  • The only permission it asks for is saving your settings.
  • Its one network request loads its own speech model, from inside the extension.
  • Audio is analysed in memory, 32 ms at a time, and never recorded or stored.
  • The code is open on GitHub, so anyone can check.

Questions

Good to know

Will it skip parts of the video?

No. It never jumps ahead. Pauses still play, just faster, and when speech starts it jumps back about 0.2 s so you hear the first syllable at normal speed.

Does it understand Hindi, or English with an Indian accent?

It doesn't need to understand words; it only listens for the sound of a human voice. The speech model was trained on many languages. Our published numbers come from English recordings, so if it misses speech in your lectures, please report the video and the time.

How much time does it save?

It depends on how often the speaker pauses. On our test talks it saved 8 to 13 seconds per 3 minutes with the balanced setting. Lectures with long writing-on-the-board pauses save much more. The aggressive setting saves more but changes speed more often.

Can I turn it off for one video?

Yes. Click the extension's icon and switch it off; the video goes back to 1× straight away.

Will it slow down my computer?

The speech model uses about 1 ms of every 32 ms of audio, on the browser's audio thread. In our tests the page stayed smooth, with no dropped frames caused by the extension.
